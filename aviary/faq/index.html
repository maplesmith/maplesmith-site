Frequently asked questions

What do I need to set up before Aviary is useful?

A home already set up in Apple’s Home app. Aviary reads that home; it is not a way to add accessories. Before installing, you want:

  • Accessories paired in the Home app and responding there.
  • A home hub - a HomePod or Apple TV - so status stays current and cameras are reachable.
  • The iPad signed in to the same iCloud account as that home.

The first time Aviary asks for home access, iOS shows its own permission prompt. If you decline it, you can change your mind later in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > HomeKit.

Why an iPad, and not an iPhone?

Aviary is designed to be read from across a room rather than held. It is landscape, full screen, and laid out for a device that sits in one place on a counter, a shelf, or a wall mount, and is looked at for a few seconds at a time. That is a different job from a phone app, and the layout would not survive the shrink. Aviary is iPad-only for that reason.

Why can I not control anything from Aviary?

That is deliberate. Aviary is a read-only display, so a dashboard left on a counter or by the front door cannot be used to unlock a door, disarm an alarm, or change the heating by anyone who walks past it. Aviary shows you the state of your home; the Apple Home app changes it.

What do I see if I have no accessories yet?

Aviary still runs. The Weather and Clock widgets work on any iPad with an internet connection and have nothing to do with your home. The Smart Home and Cameras widgets show an empty state inviting you to connect Apple Home, and they fill in as soon as accessories are available. Nothing breaks, but the app is not doing its main job until there is a home to display.

Which cameras work?

Any camera that appears in Apple’s Home app and streams there will stream in Aviary, because Aviary uses the same Apple Home connection. A few limits are worth knowing up front:

  • A camera that only works in its manufacturer’s own app, and never appears in the Home app, cannot be displayed.
  • Aviary shows live video. It does not record, store, or play back recorded clips, and it is not a way to review HomeKit Secure Video history.
  • Streams are pulled over your network, so camera feeds need the iPad and the cameras to be reachable from each other.
  • How many feeds run smoothly at once depends on your iPad, your cameras, and your network. You choose which cameras are visible in Aviary settings.

Does my home data leave the iPad?

No. Accessory status and camera video are read on the device and displayed, and are never sent to Maplesmith or anyone else. Weather, app security, analytics, and crash reporting do use the network, and the privacy policy sets out exactly what each one receives.

How is this different from Perch Home?

Perch Home is the family hub: calendars, photos, guest Wi-Fi, weather, and the time. Aviary is the smart home view: accessory status, cameras, weather, and the time. They are sibling apps from the same studio and share the same design, and you can run both on separate iPads if you want both jobs done well.

When can I get it?

Aviary is still in development and is not on the App Store yet. Email support@maplesmith.dev if you would like to hear when it is released.
